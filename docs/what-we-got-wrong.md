# What we got wrong

[Version française](what-we-got-wrong.fr.md)

Here, in date order, are the claims that were withdrawn, refuted or corrected in public, followed by our own ideas that were tested and lost. Each entry gives the date, the record, what was wrong, and how it was caught. Where the sources used here do not say how an error was noticed, the entry says so rather than guessing. DOIs have the form `10.5281/zenodo.<number>`.

They are not hidden in a footnote: they are part of the story, and often the most useful part.

## Errors caught by the method before publication

- **A forged axiom report** (behind [23653827](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23653827)). The auditor showed that the project's own certificate tool could be made to report `verified: true` for a file containing a false axiom, using a command (`#exit`) that stops Lean before the check runs. *Caught by* the auditor's deliberate attack. The tool was fixed.
- **A page that never went before the judge** (behind [23661381](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23661381)). A declaration stored without kernel checking passes the usual axiom list. *Caught by* a deliberate forgery made by the auditor. Since then the rules require a kernel replay and a search for such options.
- **A false axiom inside a claimed file** (work behind [23653850](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23653850)). A planted test axiom sat inside a file that others would import, and they would have inherited it. *Caught by* the verifier. Planted tests now live in separate files.
- **A wrong preregistration** ([23660071](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23660071)). One preregistered sub-claim was false. *Caught by* the Lean kernel itself, while formalising it. The paper reports it.

## Our own ideas, tested and refuted

These are not withdrawals. They are ideas of the project that were tested as planned and lost, and they were published as results.

- The Lieb–Thirring inequality in matrix form: a null result ([23245266](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23245266)).
- Refuted predictions kept in the conductance series (23228685, 23241463, 23244556, 23248393).
- The exciton-fluid series: most preregistered tests fail, and the papers say so.
- The zero-point idea QU-1: refuted by calculation for 8 of 12 forces ([23653833](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23653833)), then by certificate for one force ([23655392](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23655392)) and for screened forces ([23663551](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23663551)). In the first paper, the first-reported best competitor turned out to be only marginally stable and was corrected.
- The project's "envelope" formula for helium: refuted by neutron data at all 21 points of the roton region ([23653846](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23653846)).
- In biology and automated research: a certificate whose transfer to sequence data fails (23604793); chance-level thresholds that were underestimated, then corrected (23610116, 23610123); a refuted transfer to genetic programming (23611425); "recombination does not help" (23626924).
- Extending the Sandier–Serfaty approach to three dimensions: a negative feasibility result ([23655002](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23655002)). An expected quantum transition: a negative result ([23655625](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23655625)).

## Claims left without a withdrawal

A few spring records contain claims that cannot be checked from the records, and no withdrawal has been published for them. They are listed so that nobody mistakes them for results:
- the same text deposited thirteen times on 25 and 26 May (including 20380024), announcing a learning method, a 40% energy saving and "formal verification in Lean 4", in a record that contains no Lean file;
- the SymBrain (20397720) and CPUGym (20538871, 20539058) records, whose performance figures a reader cannot check.
